# Instructional Design Model

For developing my minicourse, Pain Management for EMS: Special-Case Scenarios I would pick Successive Approximation Model (SAM), particularly its lighter SAM1 variant. 

SAM's iterative, agile approach aligns well with the project's constraints: limited resources and time, a small cohort of approximately 100 learners, single-delivery execution, and the need for rapid refinement of microlearning modules.

Unlike the ADDIE model, which seeks perfection in the analysis phase, leading to delays in feedback, SAM emphasizes quick prototyping, collaboration, and continuous evaluation. SAM encourages building rough versions of case simulations and quick-reference tools, testing them early, allowing designer to incorporate feedback on ethical application, and UDL accommodations for diverse experience levels (novices vs. veterans), and refine for mobile usability.

SAM supports this minicourse through successive approximations that prioritize learner confidence and patient outcomes, while accommodating UDL's multiple means of engagement in short bursts. For instance, an initial prototype could feature a basic simulation for crush syndrome pain control. Feedback might highlight the need for more visual quick refs or audio options for on-the-go access, leading to targeted iterations rather than a full redesign. My background as a former computer science major and software programmer further reinforces this choice: Allen's parallels between instructional design and software development resonate strongly, as SAM mirrors agile methodologies I used for rapid prototyping and iterative refinement, avoiding the "waterfall" pitfalls of ADDIE where changes late in the process become costly.

SAM is not without challenges. Its reliance on ongoing collaboration could strain busy EMS partners for multiple review rounds in a resource-constrained environment, potentially slowing progress for a one-off course. While traditional models like ADDIE offer thoroughness suitable for larger programs, SAM's flexibility and speed make it the best fit for a minicourse that meets certification requirements for mastery and attendance time in a practical way.
