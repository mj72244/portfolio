# Connectivism-My Key Takeaways

Connectivism shifts the focus from the learner's internal process of learning to how learners build and navigate networks consisting of people, tools, and resources. Stephen Downes, who helped expand the idea of connectivism, stated that knowledge is "distributed across a network of connections." A personal example: just today I watched and saved Instagram reels covering why the latest American Heart Association cardiac arrest recommendations may not apply to EMS, helping new providers process their early exposures to traumatic events, and how mentors can uphold high standards without being jerks.

One concern I have is that while these kinds of learning experiences are valuable to me as an experienced paramedic, that this kind of "navigating a network" learning may not transfer well to a learner new to a particular field. As an experienced paramedic I can take these nuggets of information and integrate them with fifteen years of learning and experience easily. In contrast, despite my experience as an instructor and course developer I don't have the depth and breadth of knowledge when it comes to learning design I have difficulty evaluating and integrating knowledge in the same way. This may be generational differences (I'm a Gen X professional), but I feel that foundational education may benefit from a more traditional approach.

It comes down to understanding your audience: their background, and their experience in the field, and applying the appropriate perspective when designing learning experiences.
