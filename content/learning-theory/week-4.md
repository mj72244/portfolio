# Constructivism

## Overview

Constructivism is a learning theory that emerged in the mid-20th century. It emphasizes that learners actively build their own understanding instead of passively receiving information. Learners consider the information being taught and construct an interpretation based on their experiences, personal views, and cultural background (Kimmons, 2022).

Key researchers include:

## Lev Vygotsky (1920s–1930s)

Vygotsky introduced the concepts of the Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) and scaffolding. He argued that cognitive development occurs through culturally mediated tools, language, and collaboration (McLeod, 2025).

  

The ZPD is the gap between what a learner can accomplish independently and what they can achieve with the guidance of a more knowledgeable other. Scaffolding is where support is tailored to the learner's needs and gradually withdrawn as competence grows.

## Jean Piaget (1950s–1970s)

Piaget posited that knowledge evolves from sensory experiences to abstract reasoning through four stages of cognitive development. From childhood, we learn by actively engaging with the environment rather than passively absorbing information (McLeod, 2025).

## John Dewey (1890s–1920s)

Dewey's work aligns with Piaget's and Vygotsky's viewpoints, emphasizing active engagement by the learner with the environment to spark learning. Dewey also challenged the common view of many teachers who wanted school separated from learners' lives. He advocated connecting life experiences with school activities to make learning possible (Kimmons, 2022).

## John Keller (1980s)

Keller developed the ARCS model to provide a framework for motivating learning (University of Maryland Global Campus, 2024).

* Attention: Capture the learner's interest.
* Relevance: Promote intrinsic motivation with content applicable to the learner's environment.
* Confidence: Lay out clear goals, achievable challenges, and constructive feedback.
* Satisfaction: Provide opportunities for success and recognition.

## Implications for Learning Design

Constructivism encourages the creation of environments where learners explore, question, and collaborate. Use the ARCS model to drive intrinsic motivation by incorporating real-world problems relevant to the learner. Incorporate the ZPD to provide scaffolding tailored to the learner's needs and gradually withdraw it as competence grows.

When it comes to evaluation, constructivism encourages assessments that go beyond testing for rote memorization. Portfolios allow learners to document their learning journey. Teachers can evaluate not just the end product but the process of learning. Project-based learning tasks challenge learners to solve real-world problems, often in a collaborative environment with peers (Teachers Institute, n.d.).

## Strengths and Limitations in EMS Training

Constructivism, with its emphasis on exploration and collaboration, aligns well with EMS training. Hands-on practice with peers develops rapid decision-making, teamwork, and skill mastery under stress.

## Strengths

* Scenario-based simulations enable learners to work through real-life emergencies they will face on the job.
* Peer-led debriefs allow learners to reflect on their knowledge and performance.
* Designing simulations and skill stations with the ZPD in mind helps learners develop confidence as scaffolding is gradually withdrawn.

## Limitations

* New learners require foundational knowledge, such as anatomy or drug dosage recall, that open exploration is ill-equipped to provide.
* High-fidelity simulations and small-group activities are instructor-intensive, expensive, and challenge limited classroom resources.
