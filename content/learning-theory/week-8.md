# Minicourse Idea and Learning Theory Connection

## Pain Management for EMS: Special-Case Scenarios

This continuing education minicourse addresses a knowledge gap in delivering effective and ethical pain management in special or less-common pre-hospital scenarios, which can lead to suboptimal patient outcomes.

This continuing education minicourse is designed as microlearning modules for a total runtime of 45 minutes. This format supports busy paramedics by allowing flexible, mobile friendly delivery.

The focus is applying Maryland Medical Protocols (MIEMSS, 2025) to special cases, ensuring safe and ethical pain management.

Special cases to be considered include:

* Sickle Cell Crisis
* Burns
* Renal (kidney) stones
* Eye Trauma
* Crush or Compartment Syndrome
* Dive Injuries (Dive Medicine)
* Palliative Care for Patients with DNR/MOLST Orders

## Learning Theories

Two learning theories that will influence the development of this course are andragogy and constructivism.

## Andragogy

Malcolm Knowles assumes adult learners are experience rich, and ready to learn for real life needs (Knowles et al., 2015). A strength in this course is its emphasis on self-directed, problem-centered learning. A weakness is that the course relies on the learner's motivation to actively engage with the material. This however is not unique to andragogy. The hope is that providing relevant and engaging modules in an easy to access format would encourage active engagement.

This course will leverage paramedics current knowledge of pain management options and help deepen it by showing how pain management options can be applied in less common situations. Each module opens with a short vignette. The learner is then asked to reflect on the appropriate course of treatment and then is presented with several branching options on how to proceed. A short follow-up discussing the pros and cons of the learner's choices would be provided.

These design choices, scenario based activities, and immediate feedback align with andragogy's focus on relevance and autonomy, serving as engagement strategies and formative assessments to build confidence.

## Constructivism

Constructivist assessment encourages self reflection (Teachers Institute, n.d.). This would be facilitated by providing time for the learner to reflect on the given vignettes before being presented with the options to progress through each scenario.

John Keller's ARCS model (Attention, Relevance, Confidence, Satisfaction) would be leveraged with short compelling vignettes to capture attention and boost confidence and satisfaction by providing timely feedback on the choices the learner makes while navigating each module (University of Maryland Global Campus, 2024)

Learners will be encouraged to engage with each other in an optional online forum. This supports the social constructivism concept of the importance of active engagement in the process of knowledge construction (University of Maryland Global Campus, n.d.).

As with andragogy, constructivism assumes a motivated learner. Using the ARCS model and selecting compelling and relevant scenarios will promote learner engagement.
