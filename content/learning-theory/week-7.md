# Assessment and Learning Models

## Learning Model: Problem Based Learning

Howard S. Barrows' book Problem-Based Learning Applied to Medical Education (2000) defines problem-based learning (PBL) in medical education as an educational method designed to help students develop flexible clinical reasoning skills. Students generate hypotheses, inquire through history-taking and physical examination, and making diagnostic and treatment decisions. It uses simulations that present real patient problems, allowing free inquiry and behaviors that mirror real clinical practice.

## Learning Scenario: The Case of the Pinpoint Pupil

In this PBL activity, paramedic students are presented with a real world emergency involving a 21 year old male who is experiencing an opioid overdose complicated by hypoglycemia. The scenario unfolds during a simulated private house party. Students will need to manage both medical and social complexities.

## Learning Objectives

At the end of this PBL experience students will be able to:

* Recognize and prioritize life threats in a patient with an altered level of consciousness of unknown etiology.
* Perform primary and secondary assessments in a socially complex scene.
* Differentiate hypoglycemia, opioid overdose, seizure, stroke, and trauma as potential causes.

### Trigger

You dispatched to a private house party on a weekend night for a "21 year old unconscious male."  On arrival:

* Police are not on scene
* Approximately twenty intoxicated partygoers are present
* A distraught female meets you at the door screaming, "I think he's dying!"
* Patient is supine on the bedroom floor, unresponsive to pain, breathing six times a minute, SpO2 88% on room air, with cool dry skin

### How to Run the PBL

### Phase 1 - Initial Brainstorming

Working in small groups with a tutor acting as facilitator (not  instructor) students identify:

* What do we know?
* What do we need to know?
* Possible differential diagnosis
* Immediate life-saving interventions

Typical early differentials: opioid overdose, hypoglycemia, alcohol intoxication, seizure, or intracranial bleed.

Throughout the session the tutor provides minimal guidance, allowing students to guide the inquiry process, reflect on knowledge gaps, and to seek further information from medical journals or expert consultation. This aligns with PBL, placing the problem at the center of the learning experience and mirroring real world clinical challenges.

### Phase 2 - Self directed learning

Between sessions, students independently research:

* Differentiating causes of altered mental status
* Management of patients with altered mental status
* Management of combative or emotionally distressed bystanders

### Phase 3 - Return Session and Simulation

Students share research findings, then the tutor reveals progressive disclosures (e.g.: partial response to intranasal narcan, persistently low blood glucose, family interference). The scenario culminates in the simulation lab with a high-fidelity mannikin and actors playing the girlfriend and the angry mother who both physically interfere with patient care. Students manage the full case in real time, followed by a tutor guided debrief.

### Formative Assessment

A formative assessment for this scenario consists of ongoing group reflections and peer feedback throughout the PBL process. The tutor provides real time targeted feedback on clinical reasoning gaps without assigning grades, allowing immediate adjustment of learning.

### Authentic Assessment

Authentic assessment measures how well students can transfer skills and knowledge to realistic scenarios, focusing on what the student can do instead of just what they can recall. (New Jersey Institute of Technology, n.d.)

In this example of PBL the culminating simulation serves as the authentic assessment. Students run a complete EMS call using mannikins and monitoring equipment to manage the patient's dual conditions. It is student centered as it allows them to demonstrate skills in scenario mirroring real life EMS calls. Provided rubrics support self and peer evaluation of performance. It ties to PBL by emphasizing application of knowledge to real world problems (New Jersey Institute of Technology, n.d.).

### Connections to Learning Theories

This PBL scenario connects deeply with social constructivism (Vygotsky, 1978, as cited in University of Maryland Global Campus, 2025). The learners actively engage with the material and are active participants in constructing their understanding through collaboration and experience rather then passive reception (University of Maryland Global Campus 2025). Vygotsky highlighted the construction of knowledge and understanding through the interactions of others (University of Maryland Global Campus, 2025). The tutor provides scaffolding as students work in their Zone of Proximal Development.

These theories support PBL's focus on collaborative problem solving, making this scenario effective in developing higher order thinking in healthcare education.
