### Reflection

Designing this checklist pushed me to define what quality means before
looking at any resource. I organized it around alignment with learning
objectives, accuracy, currency, production quality, clarity,
adaptability, engagement, accessibility, and licensing. I broke
accessibility into its own sub-criteria, such as alt text, captions,
color contrast, screen reader and keyboard support, and table
structure, to provide more detailed guidance to a reviewer who may not
have a background in online accessibility issues.

Using it on *Assessment and Management of Pain in Adults*, an open
textbook from eCampusOntario, showed how it works in practice. The
resource met nearly every criterion and aligns closely with my
paramedic pain management minicourse, including how patients perceive
pain, a topic EMS education often overlooks. The checklist also made
me confirm that the CC BY-NC license fits noncommercial continuing
education before planning any adaptation.

The lesson for me is that a structured checklist turns a first
impression into a documented, repeatable decision. I'm also
considering how to capture more details about interactivity of a
resource.
