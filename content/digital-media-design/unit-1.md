### Reflection

Designing this checklist pushed me to define what quality means before looking at any resource. I organized it around alignment with learning objectives, accuracy, currency, production quality, clarity, adaptability, engagement, accessibility, and licensing, with an evidence column so every rating has to be backed up. I broke accessibility into its own sub-criteria, such as alt text, captions, color contrast, screen reader and keyboard support, and table structure, because a single yes or no hides too much.

Using it on *Assessment and Management of Pain in Adults*, an open textbook from eCampusOntario, showed how it works in practice. The resource met nearly every criterion and aligns closely with my paramedic pain management minicourse, including how patients perceive pain, a topic EMS education often overlooks. The accessibility details caught something I might have missed: low-contrast active controls. The checklist also made me confirm that the CC BY-NC license fits noncommercial continuing education before planning any adaptation.

My main lesson is that a structured checklist turns a first impression into a documented, repeatable decision. Next time I would add guidance for planning interactivity, since clinical guides like this one rarely include any.
