ADDIE

Overview 

ADDIE is an instructional design model widely used for creating effective education. It was created by the Center for Educational Technology at Florida State University for the United States Army (Boogaard, n.d.).

ADDIE is an acronym for the five phases of the methodology:

* Analysis

This is where the designer determines educational needs. In EMS education we often use another acronym: ABCD: Audience, Behavior, Condition, and Degree. We identify the audience (paramedics), behavior (applying pain management protocols in less common situations). In my experience condition and degree are more fully developed during the design phase.

Educational needs can be determined by a variety of needs. In EMS systems education requirements may be determined through quality assurance (QA) processes. QA reviews incidents to determine if medical protocols and best practices are appropriately applied. Additionally changes in protocols and introduction of new skills and equipment drive educational requirements.

* Design

The design phase involves planning the course structure, objectives, and assessments.

As the objectives are developed the condition and degree of objectives are more thoroughly mapped. Condition is the state the behavior is performed under (given a pain management scenario and access to the department's drug dosing mobile application). Finally, degree is the acceptable criteria (without drug dosing errors).

* Development

During development the actual materials are created based on the design phase. For a minicourse this may mean building slides, recording short videos, and interactive scenarios.

* Implementation

Implementation is where the course is delivered to learners. This could involve launching the course on a learning management system such as Moodle or delivering it in person in a classroom.

* Evaluation

Finally we evaluate the course's effectiveness. This can be done through feedback surveys, group discussions, or observing if the information is retained and applied. In the context of EMS, the quality assurance review helps determine the effectiveness of education.

This creates an iterative process driving the analysis of ADDIE in the creating future training.

 

Implications of ADDIE

The ADDIE model promotes a learner centered process that aligns educational content with real-world needs. By prioritizing analysis at the beginning of the process it reduces the risk of creating ineffective training.

The iterative process of evaluation helps improve educational outcomes by driving the analysis phase in the revision of existing training, and the creation of new training.

Evaluation for My Minicourse

The structured approach of ADDIE is a key strength. The analysis phase for my pain management minicourse consisted of feedback from Quality Assurance officers, evaluation of performance on the department's paramedic recertification exam, and class discussions during previous continuing education courses. These all identified an educational weakness in applying pain management protocols to less common scenarios.

A weakness is its linear approach. Other frameworks such as AGILE which engages learners in the development process may identify weaknesses in the course content earlier, allowing for course correction during development (Pappas, 2015). In the case of ADDIE, problems may not be identified until the Implementation and Evaluation phases. In the case of my minicourse, earlier engagement with learners may improve how the content is presented, or identify educational gaps not identified during the analysis phase.
