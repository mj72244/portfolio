# Learning Activities

## Minicourse Learning Outcomes

* 1. Identify key indicators and contraindications for pain management in special-case prehospital scenarios, including sickle cell crisis, burns, renal colic (kidney stones), eye trauma, crush or compartment syndrome, dive injuries, and palliative care for patients with DNR/MOLST orders, in alignment with the Maryland Medical Protocols (MIEMSS, 2025).
  2. Apply evidence-based pharmacological and non-pharmacological interventions (e.g., fentanyl, ketamine, ketorolac, positioning, and supportive care) appropriately to achieve safe and effective pain relief in uncommon scenarios while minimizing risks such as respiratory depression, hypotension, or ethical conflicts.
  3. Analyze case simulations to differentiate between standard and scenario-specific considerations for pain assessment, reassessment, and titration of analgesics, prioritizing patient comfort without delaying transport or compromising safety.
  4. Demonstrate decision-making confidence in selecting and administering appropriate pain management strategies for less-common presentations through scenario-based practice.

Maryland Institute for Emergency Medical Services Systems. (2025). Maryland Medical Protocols for Emergency Medical Services (2025 ed.) [PDF]. <https://www.miemss.org/home/Portals/0/Docs/Guidelines_Protocols/MD-Medical-Protocols-2025-Print-04282025.pdf>

# Learning Activities

  

1. Interactive Scenario-Based Case Simulations (Branching) Learners engage in 3–4 short, interactive digital scenarios where they select pain assessment tools, choose analgesics per protocols, and navigate ethical considerations (e.g., DNR orders). Immediate feedback explains correct/incorrect choices and references protocol sections. This builds confidence in decision-making for uncommon presentations. Alignment with CLOs: This activity directly supports CLO 2 (application of interventions) and CLO 3 (analysis of scenario-specific considerations) by requiring learners to choose and justify analgesics (e.g., selecting ketamine over fentanyl in crush syndrome to avoid hypotension) and navigate reassessment/titration in branching paths. For CLO 4, immediate feedback on incorrect choices build decision making confidence through repeated safe practice of uncommon scenarios.

  

2. Quick-Reference Tool Creation and Quiz Learners review a provided protocol summary infographic for special scenarios (e.g., renal colic vs. eye trauma dosing differences), then use a drag-and-drop or fill-in tool to create their own personalized quick-reference card (downloadable for field use). A short multiple-choice quiz follows to reinforce recall and application.

Alignment with CLOs: Supports CLO 1 (identifying indications/containdications) by having learners map protocol details (e.g., ketoroloc preferred for renal colic but contraindicated in trauma cases) into a personalized tool. The follow-up quiz reinforces CLO 2 application and CLO 3 differention of scenario specific dosing and reassessment.

 

3. Video Case Review with Embedded Questions Short (2–3 minute) narrated video reenactments or animations of prehospital encounters (e.g., crush syndrome or dive injury pain management) are presented, with pause-and-respond multiple-choice or short-answer questions on pain assessment, medication selection, and contraindications. Learners can rewatch sections as needed.

Alignment with CLOs: Promotes CLO 3 analysis by pausing for questions on treatment options, and CLO 2 application through selecting interventions in realistic reenactments. Rewatch option support CLO 4 confidence building for less-common cases.

  

4. Ethical Dilemma Discussion Board or Poll Learners view a brief text or audio case prompt (e.g., palliative care patient with DNR/MOLST requesting pain relief) and respond via a quick poll, reflection textbox, or optional anonymous discussion (for engagement without mandatory peer interaction). Model responses highlight protocol-aligned ethical reasoning.

Alignment with CLOs: Targets CLO 2 (minimizing ethical conflicts) and CLO 4 (decision making confidence) by reflecting on pallative/DNR cases.

  

5. Matching and Prioritization Exercise A drag-and-drop activity matches special scenarios (e.g., burns, sickle cell crisis) to appropriate first-line pain interventions, reassessment intervals, and protocol caveats from the Maryland guidelines. Followed by a prioritization ranking for multi-symptom cases.

Alignment with CLOs: Reinforces CLO 1 (contraindications/indications) and CLO 2 (appropriate interventions through matching (e.g., sickle cell crisis and fentanyl as a first-line analgesic) and prioritization in multi-symptom cases (e.g., pain control vs. resuscitation measures in crush syndrome).

6. Self-Assessment Reflection and Knowledge Check At the end, learners complete a brief reflective prompt (e.g., "Describe one change you'd make in a recent sickle cell or renal colic call based on this module") and a final 5–10 question quiz covering all scenarios. Results provide personalized review links to reinforce weak areas.

Alignment with CLOs: Synthesizes all CLOs by requiring learners to reflect on personal application and complete a comprehensive quiz building CLO 4 confidence and closing the knowledge gap.

 

These activities can be delivered via a learning management system optimized for mobile access such as Moodle, with progress tracking to meet minimum attendance requirements.
